# D8Advisr Partner Event Publishing and Change Policy

Policy ID: `partner-event-publishing-v1.0`

Version: `1.0`

Approved: 18 August 2026

Status: approved product policy; enforcement and public policy page pending

Effective date: the date database enforcement and the versioned publication
acknowledgement are released

Owner: D8Advisr

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines which event details a D8Advisr partner may change before
and after publication, how affected consumers are informed, and when D8 review
is required. Its purpose is to protect the accuracy of the promise a consumer
sees while preserving a practical route for genuine operational changes.

This policy supplements the D8Advisr Terms of Service. Applicable consumer law
and mandatory refund rights take precedence.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- events created or managed in the D8 Partner portal;
- events created or managed by a D8 administrator;
- one-off events, recurring event series, and individual occurrences;
- entry prices and any other mandatory amount required to attend; and
- changes made after an event has first been published, even if it is later
  paused, returned to draft, archived, claimed, or transferred.

## 3. Definitions

- **Draft event:** an event that has never been published.
- **Published event:** an event that has been public at least once. Publication
  is permanent historical state and cannot be erased by changing its status.
- **Published commercial baseline:** the free/paid state, price, currency, and
  disclosed mandatory charges acknowledged at first publication.
- **Free entry:** no mandatory entry or ticket fee. Optional food, drinks,
  transport, merchandise, and other clearly optional purchases may still cost
  extra.
- **Mandatory charge:** any required entry fee, booking fee, door charge,
  deposit, minimum spend, compulsory package, or external payment required to
  attend.
- **Interested consumer:** a consumer with an active reminder, saved event,
  plan inclusion, registration, ticket, or waitlist position. A page view alone
  is not interest for notification purposes.
- **Material change:** a change reasonably capable of affecting a consumer's
  decision to attend, cost, journey, eligibility, safety, or core experience.
- **Emergency change:** a necessary late change caused by safety, weather,
  venue failure, regulatory action, performer incapacity, infrastructure
  failure, or another documented circumstance outside normal planning.

## 4. First-publication acknowledgement

Before first publication, the publisher must receive a clear summary of:

- event title, date, time, location, and occurrence scope;
- free/paid state, exact entry price, currency, and mandatory charges;
- attendance mode and stated maximum, if any;
- the permanent prohibition on free-to-paid conversion and price increases;
  and
- a link to this policy.

Publication requires an affirmative acknowledgement:

> I confirm that the event's commercial details are correct. I understand that
> a free event cannot later become paid and that a published mandatory price
> cannot be increased.

D8 must store the policy ID and version, actor, organization, event, occurrence
scope, acknowledged values, and timestamp. A client-side checkbox alone is not
sufficient evidence.

## 5. Permanent published-price protections

After first publication, the following changes are prohibited indefinitely:

1. free entry to paid entry;
2. any increase above the event's current public entry price;
3. a currency change;
4. adding a new mandatory charge;
5. increasing an existing mandatory charge; and
6. using an external link, description, minimum spend, deposit, or door charge
   to obtain the same prohibited commercial result.

The price is monotonic: it may stay the same or decrease, but it may never rise
again after a reduction. The initial published price remains part of the
historical baseline, while each permitted reduction becomes the new permanent
maximum.

These restrictions apply to partners and administrators through ordinary
product workflows. Pausing, unpublishing, returning to draft, changing owner,
or transferring a listing must not reset the commercial baseline.

D8 support must not silently override the baseline. A genuine publication
mistake is resolved by cancelling the original event and creating a new event.
Interest, reminders, registrations, social proof, and historical claims do not
automatically transfer to the replacement.

## 6. Equal-or-better commercial changes

The following changes are permitted:

- reducing a paid entry price; and
- changing a paid event to free entry.

They require:

- a warning showing the current and proposed values;
- affirmative organizer confirmation;
- an immutable revision and audit record;
- notification to interested consumers; and
- financial reconciliation before the public change becomes effective once D8
  supports paid registrations.

When D8 processes payments, paid-to-free changes require a full entry-price
refund for existing purchasers. A smaller reduction requires treatment defined
by the purchase terms and applicable law; D8 should prefer automatically
refunding the difference to avoid unequal treatment.

## 7. Operational changes

### More than 72 hours before start

Low-risk corrections may apply automatically with an audit record. Material
date, time, location, capacity, eligibility, organizer, or core-experience
changes follow the enforcement matrix and may require D8 review.

### Between 72 and 24 hours before start

Commercial protections remain absolute. Material operational changes require a
reason, expedited D8 review, and immediate notification to interested
consumers. Minor corrections may still apply automatically when they do not
change the consumer promise.

### Less than 24 hours before start

Only cancellation, postponement, and documented emergency operational changes
are permitted. Commercial, identity, and promotional changes remain frozen.

### After start

The published event record is historical. Only status updates, safety notices,
incident records, and administrator corrections may be added. Corrections must
not rewrite the terms under which consumers planned, registered, or purchased.

All time windows use the event location's timezone.

## 8. Capacity

- `0` means open attendance with no listed ceiling.
- A positive number is a maximum, not live ticket inventory.
- Capacity may never be reduced below confirmed registrations.
- Capacity increases are normally low risk, but consumers on a waitlist may be
  notified when additional access becomes available.
- Open-to-limited changes and substantial reductions are material changes.
- D8 must not claim spots remaining until a real occurrence-level registration
  or ticket source exists.

## 9. Date, time, location, and core experience

Interested consumers must be notified when the date, start time, venue,
address, eligibility, accessibility, or core experience changes.

- Minor time corrections may notify without reconfirmation.
- A different date, substantial time change, different venue or area, removed
  accessibility, or materially different experience requires reconfirmation.
- A city change is not an ordinary edit. The organizer must cancel and create a
  new event unless D8 approves an emergency relocation.
- A substantive replacement of the organizer, headliner, primary activity, or
  promised experience is reviewed as a material identity change.

## 10. Cancellation, postponement, and emergency changes

An organizer may cancel or postpone when necessary, including inside a freeze
window. They must provide an accurate reason and communicate promptly.

D8 must:

- stop new discovery, registration, and plan additions as appropriate;
- preserve the event and change history;
- notify interested consumers immediately;
- show a clear cancelled or postponed status publicly;
- require a new date before treating a postponed event as rescheduled; and
- apply refund and transfer obligations when ticketing exists.

Repeated avoidable late changes, misleading reasons, or attempts to make a
consumer cancel in order to evade organizer responsibility may result in
restricted publishing, suspension, or removal.

## 11. Recurring events

Commercial protection applies to the published series and each published
occurrence.

- A free series cannot make a future occurrence paid under the same listing.
- A paid series may reduce future prices or make future occurrences free.
- Changes must specify one occurrence, a date range, or all future occurrences.
- Historical occurrences remain immutable.
- A different commercial model requires a new series.

## 12. Notifications and reconfirmation

Durable notifications are required for:

- price reduction or paid-to-free conversion;
- material capacity reduction or open-to-limited conversion;
- date, substantial time, venue, address, eligibility, accessibility, or core
  experience changes;
- approval or rejection of a partner revision;
- postponement, rescheduling, or cancellation; and
- any change requiring consumer reconfirmation.

Critical changes use in-app delivery and email when email delivery is enabled.
Push may be added later. A notification must reference the durable revision
that generated it and be idempotent across delivery retries.

Free-to-paid changes and price increases do not generate consumer change
notifications because the attempted mutation is rejected. The organizer sees a
policy-block message and the attempt is recorded for abuse monitoring.

## 13. Review and enforcement

D8 uses three lanes:

- **Blocked:** deterministic commercial violations rejected by the database.
- **Automatic:** low-risk or equal-or-better changes applied with confirmation,
  audit, and notifications where required.
- **Reviewed:** material operational, identity, late, emergency, or suspicious
  changes requiring an administrator decision.

Review is not a route around a blocked commercial change. D8 may warn, restrict,
suspend, or remove partners that repeatedly submit misleading details, evade
mandatory-charge rules, or create replacement events to preserve deceptive
interest or social proof.

## 14. Policy changes and versioning

Each accepted policy version remains attached to the publication record. A new
version does not silently replace the version previously acknowledged.
Material policy changes require a new partner acknowledgement before their next
publication or other affected action.

product decisions, not claims that every referenced platform uses those exact
rules.
